# Product thinking kernel

Use these questions before selecting a theory or framework. Record short answers in project documents; do not produce a framework essay.

1. Who has the problem, in what context, and what do they do today?
2. What observable evidence shows the problem's frequency, severity, or cost?
3. What changed, and why is solving it now better than doing nothing?
4. What outcome matters to the user, customer, payer, operator, and affected non-user?
5. Which assumption must be true for the solution to create that outcome?
6. What is the strongest alternative, including manual workarounds and no action?
7. What is the smallest valuable and reversible test?
8. What would falsify the problem or solution thesis?
9. What adoption, switching, trust, accessibility, or operational friction may prevent value?
10. Which outcome metric and guardrail metric will decide whether to continue, change, or stop?

## Definition of quality

Product quality is not feature count. It is evidence that a real problem is addressed by a coherent, usable, feasible, viable, responsible solution whose claims can be tested and whose risks are visible.
